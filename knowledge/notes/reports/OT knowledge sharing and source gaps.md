# Fill the device gaps before adding tools

**Verdict: a knowledge graph is overkill for this knowledge base, and Obsidian is a fine personal reader but the wrong way to share it.** The 68 entries are about 223 KB of text, roughly 56,000 tokens. That fits in one LLM context, and not one entry links to another, so Obsidian's graph view and backlinks would show 68 unconnected dots. The useful middle step is a generated `CROSSREF.md` table (OWASP OT item by source, superseded entries, later ATT&CK for ICS technique by source), which every audience can read. The bigger problem is content, not tooling. The knowledge base has **zero advisories** for its own reference device, the Phoenix Contact AXC F 2152, although at least six PLCnext advisories appeared in 2025-2026. Its change notes stop one edition short (en_51 against en_52). It has no source for OIDA, the tool the project's notes name as the active test engine, and this round found no public trace of OIDA. Safety-system guidance, the German operator rule TRBS 1115-1, the BSI ICS publication set and peer-reviewed scan-fragility evidence are also missing. The 57 verified feeds in the OPML file and a routine of about three hours a month will keep the base current once these holes are filled. Several of the most important fills (blocked vendor pages, paywalled standards, the OIDA origin) need the researcher to act in person.

**Evidence discipline.** **Established** marks what a source states. **Inference** marks judgment by this report or the research notes. "Local check" means files in `/home/greberl/ot-pentesting/` were read directly on 2026-11-10. Knowledge-base entries are cited by file name and were all accessed 2026-11-09. Every web URL that is new in this round was accessed **2026-11-10**. The verification method behind the "Verified" column is HTTP status plus page title (curl), WebFetch of content, or direct parsing of CSAF JSON. In that column, "yes" means the content or title was confirmed, "excerpt" means only a search snippet or secondary page was seen, and "no" means the source could not be reached.

## Sixty-eight unlinked entries make a graph tool pointless today

**Established.** Obsidian is free for commercial use, needs no account and stores data locally ([Obsidian license](https://obsidian.md/license), accessed 2026-11-10). As a team channel it is constrained. Shared Sync vaults allow **at most 20 collaborators**, and every collaborator needs an active Sync subscription. "Fine-grained permissions are not supported yet", so there is no read-only role ([Obsidian help, shared vaults](https://obsidian.md/help/sync/collaborate), accessed 2026-11-10). The pricing page lists Sync at $4 per user per month billed annually and Publish at $8 per site per month ([Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing), accessed 2026-11-10). That conflicts with the 2023 announcement of $8 or $16 per month ([Obsidian blog](https://obsidian.md/blog/new-sync-plans/), accessed 2026-11-10); the live page is the more current source. Publish hosts notes on Obsidian's servers. The Git plugin's README warns that "The Git implementation on mobile is very unstable!" ([obsidian-git](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vinzent03/obsidian-git/master/README.md), accessed 2026-11-10). A local check confirmed **0 inter-entry links** among the 68 entries; the only internal links sit in `INDEX.md`. It also found `published: unknown` in **25 of 68** entries. Obsidian assigns one property type per name "across your vault" ([Obsidian Properties](https://obsidian.md/help/properties), accessed 2026-11-10), so a Date-typed `published` would make those 25 values invalid. On graphs, Microsoft's GraphRAG README calls indexing "an expensive operation" and says the project "is largely in maintenance mode" ([microsoft/graphrag](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/graphrag/main/README.md), accessed 2026-11-10). GraphRAG-Bench (ICLR 2026) notes that "GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks" ([arXiv 2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690), accessed 2026-11-10). Anthropic advises putting a corpus under about 200,000 tokens directly into the prompt ([Anthropic, Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Logseq is splitting into a SQLite version and a Markdown version, and its two-way Markdown round-trip is "not yet shipped" ([Logseq forum, 2026-05-16](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/whats-new-with-logseq-db-may-16th-2026/35020), accessed 2026-11-10). Quartz turns Markdown into a site with backlinks, graph view and search, and is Obsidian-compatible ([Quartz](https://quartz.jzhao.xyz/), accessed 2026-11-10).

**Inference: what each audience should get.** The earlier report's storage recommendation stands: a private Git repository of Markdown, with owner, review-by date and TLP label on each page, a sanitisation gate, a forge for reading, and a static site, a TLP:GREEN export or heavier tools only when a named trigger occurs. This round **confirms** it audience by audience. The researcher's own team works in the Git repo. Anyone who likes Obsidian opens a clone as a vault with wikilinks turned off and `.obsidian/` uncommitted. Other internal security teams get read access by named group to the rendered forge, plus `INDEX.md` and `CROSSREF.md`. OT engineers will not use Git. For them the reading unit is a playbook per OWASP OT item or a device profile, shown as a rendered page or later a static site, never a vault. Other pentesting teams receive only a TLP:GREEN export after legal review, never a shared Sync vault. Obsidian Sync fails every external audience: AMBER content would sit outside company access control, with no read-only role, no review gate and a 20-person ceiling. Obsidian Publish is ruled out for AMBER content altogether.

**What this round corrects.** The earlier verdict, "skip knowledge graphs until something queries the links", was right in outcome but gave no testable trigger. The concrete trigger is a recurring question that a generated table cannot answer. The earlier mention of Obsidian's graph and backlinks also needs a caveat: both are empty until playbooks cite entries, so do not sell Obsidian to the team on those features. One size figure also changes. The earlier report measured 372 KB on disk, about 90,000 tokens. This round's local check counted 223 KB of entry text (the research notes say 234 KB). All three figures sit well under the 200,000-token mark.

**What this round adds.** Extend the existing `build_index.py` (stdlib only) to write `knowledge-base/CROSSREF.md`. That file is the graph as tables. A ten-line prototype in the research notes already produced per-item counts from the current frontmatter. OWASP OT #2 has 39 sources (31 primary) and #3 has 30, while #7 has 15 sources (12 primary) and #1 has 16 (10 primary). That skew is itself a gap signal. Replace `published: unknown` with an empty or absent value, which helps every YAML consumer, not only Obsidian. Add an optional `found_via` field so each entry records which feed surfaced it. Write the domain edges down as frontmatter IDs now (`attack_ics`, `cwe`, playbook `sources`), because they stay portable to any graph later. Two Obsidian behaviours remain **untested**, since no Obsidian install was available: whether Bases filters work on the integer flow lists in `owasp_ot`, and whether the property editor rewrites flow lists into noisy block-style diffs. Check both in ten minutes before anyone commits a shared `.base` file.

### 4. Safety systems and TRBS 1115-1 are missing from scoping

**Established.** TRBS 1115 Teil 1 ("Cybersicherheit für sicherheitsrelevante MSR-Einrichtungen") addresses employers and operators under BetrSichV. Its amended version has applied since **2026-01-15** and adds an Anhang 2 on protected equipment. These facts rest on secondary pages, because the BAuA primary text returned 403 ([activemind.legal](https://www.activemind.legal/de/guides/trbs-1115-teil-1/), accessed 2026-11-10). HSE OG86 covers industrial automation and control systems, including safety instrumented systems, at COMAH sites; the HSE page resolves, but the old OG86 PDF returns 404 ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/eci/cyber-security.htm), accessed 2026-11-10). IEC 61511 is paywalled, and ISA TR84.00.09 could not be verified. **Inference.** A pentest that touches safety-relevant control equipment in Germany meets an operator obligation that belongs in the rules-of-engagement template next to § 303b StGB. OWASP OT #4 and consequence-based reporting both need a safety-to-cyber bridge that the base does not yet provide. Any legal reading of TRBS 1115-1 needs review by counsel.

> **Update 2026-11-10 (user decision).** No peer-reviewed proof is needed for the passive-first rule. It is a project policy that follows the operator's duty of care and is backed by practitioner guidance (knowledge-base/articles-talks/infosecwriteups-ot-pentest-methodology.md; knowledge-base/bsi-germany/bsi-ics-security-compendium.md, p.99, p.106): on production, Purdue **Level 0** gets no direct contact or testing, and **Level 1** only actions known to be non-destructive, because any discovery step could disrupt the process. On a **testbed**, aggressive discovery, exploitation and fuzzing are expected, because that is what an attacker would do. The paragraph below is kept for reference only.

### 5. The passive-first rule lacks its own peer-reviewed evidence

**Established.** Pospíšil et al. tested Nmap and Zmap against PLCs on a testbed. Zmap cut off device communication, and Nmap caused delays and occasional outages ([VUT record](https://dspace.vut.cz/items/4ca50ea0-14b7-4237-a0b8-14d2f36464ad), excerpt only, accessed 2026-11-10). A related SpaCCS 2020 paper compares five scanners on seven PLCs, but it is paywalled ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-68884-4_46), accessed 2026-11-10). In the base, OWASP's warning that "there are many PLCs that can crash" from ordinary scans is the only first-hand statement ([owasp-ot-top-10-2025.md](../knowledge-base/owasp/owasp-ot-top-10-2025.md), p.19, accessed 2026-11-09). The talk it rests on is metadata-only ([youtube-plcs-to-scan-or-not.md](../knowledge-base/articles-talks/youtube-plcs-to-scan-or-not.md), accessed 2026-11-09). **Inference.** The project's strictest rule, read-only on live OT and stricter than NIST, currently rests on an OWASP sentence and one 2022 arXiv study. Reading the Pospíšil papers in full, plus BSI's test recommendations (gap 6), would turn a policy choice into a cited one. The BSI set ranks just below the top five only because it strengthens the method rather than correcting it.

**Inbox step.** Every candidate goes first into one file, `research_notes/inbox.md`, one line each: `- [ ] 2026-11-10 | <url> | <found via> | <why> | OWASP OT #?`. At each monthly pass, every line ends as *promoted* (new entry), *merged* (a fact added to an existing entry) or *dropped* (with a one-word reason). Processed lines are deleted. A line still open after two monthly cycles is dropped.

**Triage rule.** Promote a candidate to the knowledge base only if all six conditions hold. First, it is the **primary** source: when news points at an advisory, paper or repository, promote the original and record the news item in `found_via`. Second, it is **relevant**: it maps to an OWASP OT item, or to the AXC F 2152, PLCnext or CODESYS stack, or it changes an existing fact. Third, it is **recent**: published or updated within 24 months, unless it is a foundational standard or dataset. Fourth, its URL is **durable**: an advisory ID, DOI, arXiv ID or tagged release, not a live dashboard. Fifth, it is **not a duplicate**: check `INDEX.md` first, and update the existing entry instead. Sixth, it is **clean**: no leaked or pirated copies and no exploit-trading forums, and vendor marketing is recorded as `trust: secondary`.

## Conclusion

The question "Obsidian or a knowledge graph?" turned out to be the wrong lever. The tooling decision is cheap and reversible. The content decision is not. A knowledge base about testing one PLC that holds no advisory for that PLC, and whose active engine cannot be located, misleads in a way no viewer can fix. The CROSSREF table is worth building less as a sharing tool than as a gap detector. The prototype count already shows OWASP OT #7 (segmentation, 15 sources) and #1 (asset inventory, 16) sourced at half the depth of #2 (39) and #3 (30), although the earlier report made #7 the organising axis of the assume-breach checks.

A second implication concerns who does the work. This round's automated checks hit a consistent wall: BAuA, OPC Foundation, Rockwell, Armis, Otorio, INL, EUR-Lex, PTES and the vendor product page all blocked or timed out, and the normative standards are paywalled. The remaining gaps are therefore increasingly ones that only a human with a browser, a membership or a purchase order can close. The scouting routine should budget for that rather than assume feeds will deliver it.
